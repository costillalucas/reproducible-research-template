New capture from Friday the 25th, red only, and now with no-sample photos and dark frames for correction. Same four criteria, fixed before looking. It predicts the photos it did not see with error 0.52, against 0.99 without reconstructing. Number 3: by eye the grid pattern almost disappears, but the number did not drop by half, which was the requirement. Number 4: the new particles repeat in both halves, but they are phase within what the objective already resolves. The criteria were calibrated beforehand with a simulation of this same capture. It is the first real reconstruction that looks like the sample: you see the particles and their phase, which no photo records. But the central test still fails: if we reconstruct separately with half of the LEDs and with the other half, they agree up to the objective's limit and beyond it they do not agree at all. In other words: we recover the phase, we do not gain resolution. Three things from that afternoon serve as lessons about agents: not even the simulation passed the thresholds written in advance, and they had to be corrected before touching the real data, with that written down; the agent team's own hypothesis (that the grid came from not weighting each photo by its light) was tested and turned out false, because weighting makes it worse; and the grid pattern came partly from how the reconstruction was started.
