Last capture, Monday the 28th in the evening. We changed two things at once: the matrix went from 74 to 98 millimetres, which raises the overlap between neighbouring LEDs from 31 to 46 percent, and each colour was focused separately. The agents computed each LED's exposure time from the previous capture, and it was corrected on the fly with what was being measured. Result: all three colours predict the photos they did not see, with error between 0.46 and 0.62, against 0.98 without reconstructing; red gives the best value of the project. By eye, the three colours reconstruct the same particles in the same places; in the previous take red and blue were blobs. The two halves of LEDs agree in the fine detail between 5 and 14 times more than by chance, but still below the threshold we set. In other words: combining the colours now makes sense, but the resolution gain is not shown yet. And since we changed two things together, we do not know which helped more.
