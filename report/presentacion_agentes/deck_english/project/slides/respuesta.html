Give the conclusion up front so everyone knows where the talk is going. What was achieved is mostly infrastructure and auditing. What was not achieved is the final scientific goal: a real image with more detail that we trust. The red reconstruction from Friday the 25th is the first one that looks like the sample, but it still shows no new detail. The five errors: in our program, the frozen solver, the scale of the initial image and the uncorrected exposure; in the data, the reference image without detail and the wrong objective in the configuration. The three problems in Lucas's earlier code are separate. The centre of the matrix was measured from the images; the height z cannot be pinned down from the images, so the hand measurement (74 mm) was used. On resolution: the spheres are large and a single photo already separates them, but the two-halves test does not need small objects; in simulation with similar particles it passes and on real data it does not, so something in the model or the data is also failing. Important distinction: predicting photos the reconstruction did not use proves that it recovers the real phase of the sample (and the test can fail: it failed in the zone with volume scattering). It does not prove a resolution gain, because each unseen photo shares much of its spectrum with its neighbours; that is what the two-halves test measures, and it does not pass yet.
