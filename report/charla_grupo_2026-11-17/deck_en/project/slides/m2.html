Resolution: the aperture rules

(1.5 min) Read the figure row by row. A detail of period p deflects light at sin θ = λ/p, i.e. it appears in the spectrum at ±1/p. The objective only lets through the frequencies inside a circle of radius NA/λ: the pupil. 20 µm fringes: the peaks are inside, the camera sees them. 9 µm: the peak falls right on the edge, it passes partially and the contrast drops. 5 µm: the peaks fall outside and the picture comes out flat. Minimum period in the coherent case: λ/NA (do NOT give the number here: the audience computes it in the next slide, question 1; it is ≈ 9 µm); in the incoherent case it would be λ/(2NA), because the camera measures intensity and its spectrum extends to 2NA/λ. FPM works in the coherent case: each LED is almost a plane wave because its angular size (d/z) is much smaller than the NA. In the raw photo, the 'isolated' objects of the dense 500 nm sample are ~7 µm wide (FWHM; coherent theory for the objective NA: 6.6 µm). Careful: Rayleigh, Abbe and FWHM give different numbers; when comparing, use the same criterion.
