(2 min) Leave 30–60 s: ask for a show of hands for A, B, C, D on each question before clicking. Question 1, correct C (9 µm): the coherent cutoff is NA/λ, so the minimum period is λ/NA = 0.63/0.07 = 9 µm. Distractors: A, 4.5 µm = λ/(2NA), the incoherent limit (see below); D, 0.11 = NA/λ, the formula inverted (that is the cutoff frequency in µm⁻¹, not a period); B is a factor 10 off. Question 2, correct B (0.45): NA = λ/p = 0.63/1.4 ≈ 0.45, about 6 times our objective. Distractors: C, 2.2 = p/λ, the formula inverted, and impossible in air (NA cannot exceed 1, slide 3); A, 0.22 ≈ λ/(2p), the incoherent answer; D is a factor 10 off. Why 9 and not 4.5: with one LED the illumination is a single plane wave, so the image is coherent: the objective filters the FIELD, whose spectrum has the zero order at the centre of the pupil and the ±1 orders at ±1/p; the fringes need a first order inside the pupil, 1/p ≤ NA/λ. With incoherent (or extended, filled-condenser) illumination the camera records intensities from many angles at once, the transfer function is the autocorrelation of the pupil and reaches 2NA/λ: zero order at one edge, first order at the opposite edge, hence λ/(2NA). That is exactly what FPM does on purpose, one oblique LED at a time.
