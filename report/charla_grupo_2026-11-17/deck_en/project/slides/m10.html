(1.5 min) A result using the "spheres smaller than the resolution" test: the image of each sphere is the system's response, and its width tells us what resolution we have. With 30 spheres of 500 nm in red: the raw photo gives 6.4 µm (NA ~0.07, that of the objective). The null, with the dark field shuffled, gives 4.7–5.5 µm: it also improves, because it keeps the bright-field LEDs (the on-axis one and its four neighbours), which already widen the aperture a little. The FPM reconstruction gives 3.0–3.2 µm (effective NA ~0.14, with NA_eff ≈ 0.705·λ/FWHM for coherent imaging). Honest reading, step by step: from 6.4 to ~5 µm is explained by bright field; from ~5 to ~3 µm is contributed by dark field, and that is the real gain (~1.6×) because it disappears in the null; from ~3 to ~1 µm (what a synthetic NA of 0.45 would allow) is missing. Whether that is achievable with this setup we do not know yet: it is the model error from the open questions. Note: 3 µm is still far above 0.5 µm, so full super-resolution has not been demonstrated. Pitfalls of this test: the spheres must be isolated, and the width depends on the criterion (FWHM, Rayleigh).
