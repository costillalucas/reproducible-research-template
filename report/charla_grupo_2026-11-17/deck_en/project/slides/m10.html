(1.5 min) Red capture of 21/10, a dense sample of 500 nm polyamide spheres. The idea of the test: an object smaller than the resolution shows the system's response, and its width tells us the resolution. In the raw bright-field photo we picked 34 objects that look isolated (nothing bright within 10 µm) and measured the width at half maximum (FWHM) of their averaged, centred profile, in the same way for the raw photo, each half of the checkerboard and the null. Raw photo: 7.0 µm (the coherent theory for the objective NA gives 6.6 µm). Null, with the dark-field photos shuffled: 5.3 µm in one half and 7.9 µm in the other, so no consistent narrowing. FPM, each half: 3.0 and 3.2 µm. Honest reading: the objects become 2–3 times narrower only when the real dark field is used, and the same shapes appear in both halves and not in the null. But in the reconstruction these "isolated" points are aggregates of several spheres (crosses, Y shapes, chains), not single spheres, so this is not a clean PSF and we do not quote a resolution factor or an effective NA from it. That same capture fails C2 globally (0.011 against 0.009 in the null): the model does not predict this dense sample well. Also, ~3 µm is still far from the ~1 µm that the synthetic NA of 0.45 would allow, and far above 0.5 µm. Next step for a clean test: diluted spheres, separated by more than 15–20 µm.
