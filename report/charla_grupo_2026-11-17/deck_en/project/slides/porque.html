Motivation

A cheap objective sees a wide field, but little detail

FPM keeps the large field of the low-NA objective and recovers the detail from varying illumination with an LED array and an algorithm, with no moving parts, and it also gives us the phase of the sample.

FPM Mini-course · simulation: synthetic absorbing object, no noise; the FPM panel is an ideal image with NA 0.45, not a reconstruction

(1 min) Statement of the problem with the same simulated field of "cells" in the three panels. With our 2.5× (NA 0.07) the whole 1.4 mm field is visible, but the smallest detail is around 4.5 µm: in the zoom the cells are blobs. With a 10× (NA 0.25) the detail reaches ~1.3 µm, but only 1/16 of the area is visible (the blue box). The goal of FPM is to have both: full field and ~1.4 µm detail with a synthetic NA of 0.45. In numbers: δ = λ/(2NA) gives 4.5 µm with NA 0.07 and 0.70 µm with NA 0.45, i.e. about 41 times more distinct details in the same field. Make clear that the third panel is an ideal simulated image, not a result. For diagnosis (for example blood smears) this means many cells per picture with sufficient detail.
