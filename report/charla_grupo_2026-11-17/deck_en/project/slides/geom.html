Which LEDs give bright field? Compare sin θ with the NA

(1 min) It prepares the next slide. Step 1, pure geometry: an LED at distance r from the axis, with the array at height z, illuminates the sample at an angle θ such that sin θ = r/√(r² + z²). Step 2, the rule: the objective accepts light up to sin α = NA. If sin θ < NA, the direct light of the LED enters the objective and the picture has a bright background: bright field. If sin θ > NA, the direct light passes outside and only the light the sample diffracts enters: dark field, only bright edges. Example: an LED at 12 mm with z = 100 mm gives 0.119 > 0.07, so it is dark field. With our array (pitch 6 mm, z ≈ 100 mm): the cross neighbors at 6 mm give sin θ ≈ 0.060 < 0.07, bright field; the diagonals at 8.5 mm give ≈ 0.085 > 0.07, dark field. This is what the next slide shows. Equivalent in the setup: the circle where the acceptance cone intersects the array has radius z·tan α ≈ 7 mm. The Fourier calculations (k = sin θ/λ) are in the appendix.
