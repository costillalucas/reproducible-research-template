It is not the experiment

(1 min) First half of the tree: what could be wrong in the experiment. Noise (real data, 13/11): doubling the exposure raises the signal-to-noise ratio ×1.8 in the dark-field rings, and C1 stays the same (0.728 versus 0.734). Magnification (real data): the photo of the millimetre ruler (100 µm divisions, one every 77.5 px; measured again on the raw image: 1.289 µm/px) gives 1.290 µm per pixel at the sample, magnification 2.48; the nominal 2.28× is excluded, and using it worsens the prediction by +0.069. Array position: rotating it ±1° (real data, 23/10) changes C1 by ~0.001, and tilting it 1–3° without modelling it (simulation, 28/10) by +0.007 to +0.013; everything below the normal difference between the two halves (~0.02). Conclusion: the problem is not in the data acquisition or in the coarse geometry of the setup. Detail: the rotation comparison is at 14 epochs in both runs.
