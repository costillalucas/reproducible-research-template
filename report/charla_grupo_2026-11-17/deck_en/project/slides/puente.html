Mini-course · 2 · Illumination and Fourier

From the LED array to Fourier space, in three steps

(1.5 min) The previous slide went from the data to the numbers: we measured the radius and the shift in the FFT. This one goes the other way: from the geometry of the setup to Fourier space, which is what the algorithm needs to place each photo. Step 1: with the LED at distance r from the axis and the array at height z, sin θ = r/√(r² + z²). Step 2: why k = sin θ/λ. The wavefronts of the tilted wave are separated by λ; on the sample, their trace repeats every λ/sin θ (triangle between wavefront and sample). That is: tilted illumination is a phase "grating" of period λ/sin θ resting on the sample, with frequency sin θ/λ. Multiplying the sample by that phase ramp shifts its spectrum by k = sin θ/λ (shift theorem). It is the same relation as on slide 6 read backwards: there a grating of period p deflects light at sin θ = λ/p (frequency → angle); here the tilted LED turns angle → frequency. The pupil does not change, it has radius NA/λ. Step 3: if sin θ < NA, the zero order (the direct light) falls inside the pupil and the photo has a bright background: bright field; if sin θ > NA, it falls outside and only the light the sample diffracts gets in: dark field, only bright edges. Worked example: LED at 12 mm, z = 100 mm → sin θ ≈ 0.119, k ≈ 0.189 µm⁻¹, and since 0.119 > 0.07, dark field. Do not solve the 6 mm LED: it is the next question, for the audience.
