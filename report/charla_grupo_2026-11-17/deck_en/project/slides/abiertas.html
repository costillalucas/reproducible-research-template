(1.5 min) Three doubts, each with the experiment that answers it. 1) Is the limit the model? The ruled-out causes left a non-geometric model error alive: the real residual (~0.68) is far above the simulated one (0.15). Candidates: stray light (~30 % in the flat), different brightness of each LED, pupil shape. Experiment: measure them and add them to the model, and see whether C1 drops. 2) Is the super-resolution real? The 5 µm spheres are at the limit of the objective and the 0.5 µm ones only give a width; without an object of known size it cannot be shown directly. Experiment: 2–3 µm spheres or diffraction gratings (100/300/600 lines per mm). The LEC has no USAF target. 3) Does more overlap help? This is the upcoming experiment: move the array away to z ≈ 143 mm (next slide). It also tests a suspicion: C2 compares two checkerboard halves, which have less overlap than the full grid; if C2 improves much more than C1 when the array is moved away, C2 was pessimistic. Another minor doubt, about calibration: the pupil edge went from 7.1 to 7.4 mm between 30/10 and 03/11 with no identified cause (equivalent to ~4 mm of z). New data: the large 13/11 set (11 fields in red and regions in red, green and blue) is the basis for training a model; the green and blue flat is still missing.
