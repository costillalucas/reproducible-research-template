Mini-course · 3 · Reconstruction

Forward model and phase retrieval

(1.5 min) Reconstructing means searching for the spectrum Õ, and sometimes the pupil P, that reproduces all the photos. Walk through the cycle: 1) with the current estimate, take the window of LED j; 2) go back to real space: that is the photo the model predicts; 3) compare it with the measured one: keep the phase and replace the amplitude by √I_j; 4) go back to Fourier space and correct only inside the circle. Then the next LED, from the centre outwards. Watch the sign: with Õ(k − k_j) the window falls at −k_j; the drawing shows it at +k_j, which is only a convention. Every symbol of the model hides an assumption: thin sample, plane wave with the same k across the whole field, the same pupil across the whole field, all LEDs with the same brightness, and photos with no background or systematic noise. In our setup: EPRY (also recovering the pupil) did not improve the prediction of held-out LEDs (red 25/09: 0.529 with EPRY versus 0.517 without; in green 24/09 it worsened both training and validation). Separately, the training error alone is not a valid criterion: in the green magnification sweep it went down while the validation error went up (overfitting). References: Zheng et al. 2013 (original algorithm), Ou et al. 2013 (EPRY), Yeh et al. 2015 (amplitude cost). Three things cannot be decided because they do not change any photo: a constant global phase, a joint shift of Õ and P in k, and the NA·Δx scale.
