If the windows of neighbouring LEDs did not overlap, could we still reconstruct? What do we gain and what do we lose by moving the array farther away?

FPM Mini-course · answer in two clicks · discs to scale (NA 0.07, λ 0.63 µm, pitch 6 mm) · sampling criteria: Sun et al. 2016, Opt. Express

(2 min) The shared region appears in both photos, and the field has to explain both at once: that ties the phase of one window to that of its neighbour. If they share nothing, each piece of the spectrum can be rotated in phase without any photo changing. Second click: with LEDs of pitch p at height z, the separation between centres in sin θ is ≈ p/z. With our geometry (area shared between two neighbouring pupils) the overlap rises from 47 % at z = 99.5 mm to 62 % at z = 143 mm, but the outermost LED sits at a smaller angle: the maximum illumination NA drops from 0.52 to 0.39 and the synthetic NA from ~0.59 to ~0.46. This is exactly the decision we made for the next capture (z ≈ 143 mm). Extra point to discuss at the end: the halves we use for validation (checkerboard) have less overlap than the full grid.
