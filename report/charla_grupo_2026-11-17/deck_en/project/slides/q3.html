Overlap between neighbouring windows

1 · If the windows of neighbouring LEDs did not overlap, could we still reconstruct?

A Yes: more LEDs simply cover more spectrum

B Yes, if the windows touch edge to edge

C No: each window keeps its own free phase

D Yes, with longer exposures

2 · What do we gain and what do we lose by moving the LED array farther away?

A Gain synthetic NA, lose overlap

B Gain overlap, lose synthetic NA

B Gain overlap, lose synthetic NA

C Gain both overlap and synthetic NA

D Nothing changes: same LEDs, same angles

(2 min) Show of hands for A–D on each question, then one click per question. Question 1, correct C. The shared region appears in both photos, and the field has to explain both at once: that ties the phase of one window to that of its neighbour. If they share nothing, each piece of the spectrum can be rotated in phase without any photo changing, so the solution is not unique. Distractors: A (more coverage does not fix the free phases), B (touching edge to edge still shares nothing), D (more signal does not create a constraint). Rule of thumb: 40–60 % overlap or more. Question 2, correct B. With LEDs of pitch p at height z, the separation between window centres in sin θ is ≈ p/z: farther away, closer windows. With our geometry the overlap rises from 47 % at z = 99.5 mm to 62 % at z = 143 mm, but the outermost LED sits at a smaller angle: the maximum illumination NA drops from 0.52 to 0.39 and the synthetic NA from ~0.59 to ~0.46. A is the reverse; C is impossible (the same LEDs cannot reach larger angles from farther away); D forgets that the angles depend on z (and the light per LED also drops as 1/z²). This is exactly the decision we made for the next capture (z ≈ 143 mm). Extra point: the halves we use for validation (checkerboard) have less overlap than the full grid.
