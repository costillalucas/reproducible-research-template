Take-home

Three ideas from this talk

1

A tilted LED is a disc in Fourier space.

Overlapping discs recover the phase and build an aperture larger than the objective's.

2

New detail is proven against a null control.

It only counts if it vanishes when the dark field is destroyed.

3

Dark field already helps; the model is missing.

Structure 2–3× narrower than the raw photo, only with the real dark field; today's limit is neither noise nor geometry.

(1 min) Three take-home ideas, one for each part of the talk. First, the mini-course: in FPM each tilted LED lets a different disc of the sample spectrum through; if neighbouring discs overlap, the photos are tied to each other, that fixes the phase, and the union of all the discs is a synthetic aperture larger than the objective's. Second, validation: a reconstruction always looks sharp; new detail counts only if it disappears in the null control, when the dark-field photos are shuffled. Third, our setup: in the dense 500 nm sample the objects go from ~7 µm in the raw photo to ~3 µm, only with the real dark field (the null gives 5.3–7.9 µm); they are aggregates, not a clean PSF, so we quote no resolution factor; we ruled out noise, magnification, array position, iterations, aberrations and thickness; what remains is a model error, and the next step is to move the array away and measure what the model leaves out. Then open to questions; the appendix has the geometry-to-Fourier calculations and the rounding in k in case someone asks.
