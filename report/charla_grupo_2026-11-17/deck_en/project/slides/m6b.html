Reconstruction, iteration by iteration

Amplitude (left) and phase (right; orange +, blue −), iterations 0 to 30.

Iteration 0: √I of the central LED, at the objective's resolution.

Each iteration sweeps the 49 LEDs from the centre outwards and corrects only each LED's window.

At the end the amplitude sharpens, but part of the amplitude remains imprinted in the phase: the error does not reach zero.

Simulation with the model from the interactive Fourier page v2: incremental descent on an amplitude loss, from the centre outwards, with increasing step 0.8·(1 − e^(−0.3 t)). The global phase is aligned with the known sample only for visualization; the sample is not used in the gradient. The relative amplitude error drops from 9.2 % to 1.7 % on the mixed sample. Even without noise or calibration errors, the phase keeps a trace of the amplitude: a low loss does not guarantee that the object is exact. With real data, noise and model error are added (modules 8 and 9).
