FPM: one LED in the setup is one disk in Fourier space

(2 min) The key slide of the mini-course: everything else builds on it. On the left, our setup: a 21×21 LED array about 100 mm below the sample; one LED is switched on at a time and each one illuminates the whole sample, almost like a plane wave, from a different angle. Nothing moves. LED A (at 6 mm) illuminates at an angle smaller than the one the objective accepts: its direct light enters and the picture is bright field. LED B (at 24 mm) illuminates at a larger angle: the direct light passes outside and only the light the sample diffracts enters: dark field, with bright edges (the picture is heavily enhanced). On the right, the same in Fourier space, to scale: each picture measures a disk of the spectrum of radius NA/λ, centered where the LED shifts it. The dashed circle centered at the origin is the NA limit of the objective: if the center of an LED's disk falls inside, that LED is bright field (equivalent to its disk containing the zero order). A's falls inside by a small margin; B's, outside. All the LEDs together build the gray tiling: the synthetic aperture, ~0.58 along the axes. Neighboring disks overlap, and the algorithm stitches them together. Where the name comes from: classical ptychography (X-rays, electrons) measures a sample through windows that are shifted and overlap; FPM does the same but with the windows in Fourier space.
