FPM Mini-course · to scale: 6 mm pitch array at z = 102 mm, up to ring 7 (15 × 15 LEDs)

(1.5 min) A tilted plane wave multiplies the sample field by a phase ramp; in Fourier space, that shifts the spectrum. Since the pupil stays fixed, each LED lets through a circle of radius NA/λ at a different place in the spectrum. In bright field, the zero order enters the pupil and the picture has a bright background; in dark field it falls outside and only scattered light arrives, which is why those pictures are dim. FPM stitches all the pieces into a single spectrum, and the overlap between neighbors (40–60 % or more) is what fixes the phase. The rule: synthetic NA = objective NA + maximum illumination NA. With 15 × 15 LEDs (ring 7) it gives ~0.45; with the 21 × 21 grid of slide 4 it reaches ~0.58 along the axes. On a flat array, the outer LEDs are closer together in k than the central ones, because sin θ grows more and more slowly. The diagram is to scale (October geometry, z = 102 mm, NA 0.074 in the original): it shows how much larger the synthetic NA is than the pupil of the objective.
