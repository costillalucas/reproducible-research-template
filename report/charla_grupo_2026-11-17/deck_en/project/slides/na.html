Motivation · the key number

Numerical aperture: the cone of light the objective accepts

(1 min) Before going on we need one number: the numerical aperture. Panel 1: an objective only collects the light that leaves each point of the sample inside a cone; NA = n·sin θ, with θ the half-angle of that cone and n the refractive index between sample and lens (air: n = 1). Our 2.5× has NA 0.07: a cone of only ~4°. A 10× has NA 0.25, ~14.5°. Click 1: the NA is engraved on every objective next to the magnification ("2.5× / 0.07"). Typical values go from ~0.07 to ~0.65 in air; since sin θ cannot exceed 1, dry objectives stop around 0.95, and to go beyond you fill the gap with immersion oil (n ≈ 1.5), as in a 100×/1.25. Click 2: the price of a large cone. With the 10× instead of our 2.5×, the field on our camera drops from 1.4 mm to 0.35 mm (1/16 of the area) and the depth of field from ~130 µm to ~10 µm. In the next block we will see what a larger cone buys: finer detail. That trade-off — wide field or fine detail — is exactly what FPM tries to break. Keep the dashed cone in mind: it reappears in the next slides.
